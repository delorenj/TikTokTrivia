# Glossary

Terms as this project uses them. Several differ from their textbook meanings, and the differences are
load-bearing.

**walking skeleton** — Shipping one end-to-end video early that is deliberately bad: stills, no
animation, no captions, just a voice reading questions and answers straight through. Here it is an
antidote to impatience after the stills phase, not an architectural technique. Everything after it is
framed as polish.

**the sugar drip** (against **the fire hose**) — The delivery shape for the curriculum. Not merely
rationed information, but portions small enough to be addictive and individually useful. The failure
it corrects is named: dumping MCP servers, skills, CLI choice and model choice all at once.

**the project group** / **assign-the-tasks model** — The mental model for decomposition being taught.
Imagine the job handed to a school group of three to six people with you as project leader, and split
it the way you would split it for them. The claim: the split you would give humans *is* the task list
you give AI. Decomposition, not coding, is the AI user's actual job.

**"what does he hand you back"** — The test for whether a subtask is specified well enough to
delegate: name the concrete artifact the assignee returns. That artifact description is the prompt.
Applied to every subtask, not just the creative ones.

**the dumb one** / **the dumb kid** — Model selection framed as casting a group member. Some subtasks
go to the cheap model on purpose. Routing everything to the best model is possible and wasteful. The
joke framing is intentional and flagged as such in the source.

**MCP server** — In this curriculum, never taught as a topic. Only ever the answer to a task Carrie
already wants done: make the AI search, make it write into a spreadsheet. The name arrives after the
capability is felt.

**skill** — The vehicle for encoding taste and output format: what to look for when studying other
videos, and the shape it must report back in. Positioned as the competing candidate to MCP for the
creative-direction slot.

**Hermes agent** — What Jarad hands Carrie. A self-hosted agent with memory that he primes in advance.
It installs tools for her but narrates their provenance first, builds skills by interviewing her,
reports at session end, and pokes her between sessions. The delivery mechanism, not a chat window.

**open notebook** — The self-hosted notebook service, proposed as the accumulating record of the whole
journey. A trivia notebook Carrie can return to and add to on her own.

**BMAD** — A structured method used but deliberately hidden: baked into the agent's behavior rather
than surfaced as commands. She gets the structure without meeting the framework.

**spike** — Used for the question-research subtask specifically because its method is unknown. The
deliverable is a set of candidate sources discovered by trying, not a known procedure executed.

**the sweet spot** — The calibration band for question difficulty. See `question-calibration.md`.

**the creative director role** — The subtask that produces vision only, no media: what the video looks
like, as a descriptive screenplay plus an art-and-style description. Expected to become a reusable
personal style once enough videos exist.

**touch points** / **scenes** — The per-question units of the video, one still frame each, roughly ten
per video. The source flags that it lacks the right word for these.

**the unsaid target** — The real learning objective, never stated to Carrie directly. See
`learning-outcomes.md`.

**underline the moves** — The substrate every lesson sits on, to be locked down before sub-lessons are
authored. First and foremost: where Carrie actually types.

**failure** (of the project) — Defined against Carrie's conclusion, not the artifact. Finishing with a
real video in hand and a worse opinion of AI than she started with. Shipping does not redeem it.
