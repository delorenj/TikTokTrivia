# Learning outcomes

What Carrie is supposed to walk away holding, and how to tell whether she does. None of this is
delivered as content — it is the unsaid target, reached by doing the work. Saying it out loud turns it
into something to nod along to and discard.

**Timing.** The first read on the mindset checks is due **within a couple of lessons**, not at the
end. That is a sequencing constraint: if the amplifier realization has not started landing by then,
the pacing is wrong and the curriculum needs adjusting while there is still runway.

---

## The unsaid target

Three claims, in the order they matter:

1. **The differentiator is the person, not the model.** Hand the same setup to ten people and get ten
   different results. People using AI produce work that is excellent or terrible, and the AI is the
   constant in both cases.
2. **AI does not take your place; it amplifies whoever is running it.** The work she puts in is what
   makes the output hers. She is the boss — which carries the credit *and* the blame when the work
   comes back wrong. That second half is what defuses the project's defining failure: a weak video is
   the boss's call to redirect, not evidence against the tool.
3. **Not using it is not a viable option** against someone equally good who does. Not out of loyalty
   to the technology — out of speed and volume.

And the diagnosis that goes with them: **if this is not fun, the AI is not the reason.** Two readings,
and they lead different places. Either she has not yet learned to squeeze it — which more practice
fixes — or she does not actually enjoy the thing being made, in which case the right response is a
different subject, not more lessons. The amplifier only works on something she genuinely loves.

## Checks

Mindset — the ones that actually decide success:

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Asked what she got out of this | An answer about leverage, about what it lets her do | Reciting tools, or "I'd have to ask you again" |
| "If I gave this exact setup to ten other people, would their videos be the same?" | No — and the reason is the person's taste, ideas, choices | Better prompts, better model, or a paid tier |
| Asked to name the parts of the video that are hers | Three or more specific decisions — which questions, the theme, the voice, what she rejected | None, or "the AI made it" |
| First frustrating render, verbatim | "This part is boring" / "I don't know how to get what I want yet" | "AI sucks at this" / "this is why I don't like AI" |
| "Why not just do it yourself?" | Speed and volume | "Because AI is better than me" |

Behavior — observable during the run:

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Rejecting and redirecting output | At least one specific correction sent back per phase | Accepts everything, or abandons the phase when output is wrong |
| Instructions naming the deliverable | A majority specify an artifact by the second phase, rising | Instructions stay activity-shaped |
| Model routing | Picks a cheap model for finding questions, and can say why | "The best one," or "does it matter?" |
| Pull versus push | The between-session nudge gets more yeses than noes; at least one session starts with no nudge at all | Every session begins with Jarad asking |
| Session continuity | She names the next phase at the start of a session, before being told | "I don't remember where we were" |

Transfer — the pillar that outlives the project:

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Fresh unrelated task, split among four imaginary group members | Four distinct tasks, each with a named handback | One big task, or four restatements of the same thing |

Process — decidable from artifacts, not from Carrie:

- The skeleton cut is timestamped before the first polish work. Fail: animation, captions, or effects
  land while no full-length version exists.
- Every lesson ends with something that ran and produced an artifact she asked for. Fail: a lesson
  whose only output is understanding of a mechanism.
- A session report exists for every session.
- Instruction turns per video decline across videos 1..N. Fail: flat or rising.

Explicitly **not scored**: whether she can reproduce any procedure, and the quality of the early
videos.

## Failure modes

The named total misfire, which shipping does not redeem: **she reaches the end with a finished video
in hand, judges it bad, and generalizes that to AI being bad.**

The routes that lead there:

- **Front-loading.** Volume of interesting material is the mechanism of failure, not of teaching.
- **Impatience at the stills phase**, during the stretch where assets pile up and nothing is
  watchable yet. Anticipated explicitly; Phase 5 exists to arrive before it.
- **Frustration hardening into a verdict** about the tool rather than reading as a signal she has not
  learned to squeeze it yet — "AI is failing me," "I hate AI's results."
- **A mechanism taught with nothing useful hanging off it.** The drip stops being addictive and
  becomes a lecture.
- **Delivering the argument above out loud** as curriculum.
- **Jargon handed over raw**, or a plain-language translation that talks down to her.
- **Treating early low quality as a problem** instead of as the expected start of an iteration curve.
- **An unlocked surface** that drifts from lesson to lesson, or exposing the wider toolchain as a menu
  of choices she has to have opinions about.

---

## What is not the goal

Adoption. She may finish this and decide not to embrace it, and that is an acceptable outcome — the
target is that she can see how it would help *if* she decides to. Designing for conversion adds
pressure this project does not ask for.
